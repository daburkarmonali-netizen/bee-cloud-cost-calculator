How the math works — and what it deliberately ignores

Everything is computed client-side in plain JavaScript. No tracking, no API calls, no dependencies beyond a web font.

Subscription side

monthly × 12 × years — a straight line that never flattens. Real bills usually rise; this model assumes they never do, which favours the subscription.

Ownership side

device price + (watts ÷ 1000 × 24 × 365 × years × $/kWh)

Idle draw is modelled at 10 W and electricity at $0.17/kWh (roughly the US average), so about $15/year in power. Ignoring it would make ownership look better than it is.

Break-even

months = price ÷ (monthly − monthly power cost) — the month cumulative subscription spend passes cumulative ownership cost. If your subscription costs less per month than the electricity, it never breaks even, and the calculator says so.

Deliberately excluded

  • Drive replacement. Hardware fails; a 10-year horizon realistically includes one replacement. Add it via the custom price if you want the pessimistic case.
  • Off-site copy. Ownership alone is not a backup strategy — proper 3-2-1 means a second copy somewhere else, which may reintroduce a small recurring cost.
  • Price inflation on either side, and any resale value of the hardware.

Read it as an order-of-magnitude tool, not a quote. Subscription prices are US list prices at time of writing and vary by region and promotion. The interesting output isn't the exact dollar figure — it's how early the crossover happens.
